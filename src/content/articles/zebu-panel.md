In July 2025, we attended a panel at Zebu Live 2024 on the future of DAOs and how novel decentralised organisations are overcoming challenges to create more inclusive and efficient Web 3.0 services. Below is our executive summary of the discussions and their main takeaways.

---

## Executive Summary

* **DAO reality check** - We framed DAOs as powerful coordination tools that have advanced DeFi, gaming, and governance, while also struggling with decentralization, participation, and legitimacy. A recurring theme was the gap between the DAO ideal and the messy reality of “DAO‑in‑name‑only” structures that look like a multisig plus Discord. True decentralization was described as a direction of travel rather than a finished state.

* **Beefy’s lived lessons** - We shared how Beefy’s early governance constraints shaped its path: a fixed token model, rapid token distribution that starved contributor incentives, and a founder exit after a bull run that forced decentralization. Despite these drawbacks, the DAO grew to over 15,000 tokenholders, became revenue‑generating, and maintained active participation. That experience grounded the panel in practical tradeoffs rather than pure theory.

* **Community over apathy** - We highlighted apathy as a major hurdle during bearish cycles and argued that resilient communities are what keep DAOs alive. In Beefy’s case, a diverse community bonded not just by DAO ideology, but by shared interests in DeFi strategies, meme culture, and NFTs. We positioned decentralization as a progressive mission that must be continually nurtured through engagement.

* **Governance power imbalance** - We discussed how one‑token‑one‑vote creates plutocratic outcomes and can allow whales to dominate. Short‑term tools like quadratic voting and vote‑locking can dampen whale influence and encourage long‑term alignment, but they are not permanent fixes. The panel emphasized that governance tooling must evolve beyond simple token weight if DAOs are to scale fairly.

* **Reputation and sybil risk** - We explored reputation‑based voting as a next frontier, with identity‑aware governance potentially empowering the long tail of tokenholders. But we also cautioned that reputation systems can drift into surveillance or coercion if poorly designed. The panel stressed the need for sybil‑resistant designs, nuanced trust signals, and privacy‑preserving techniques such as zero‑knowledge proofs.

* **Automation and cybernetics** - We argued that DAOs should move past “progressive decentralization” toward progressive automation, using smart contracts to enforce rules and reduce governance capture. This vision treats DAOs as cybernetic systems that can operate with fewer discretionary interventions. The aim is higher organizational efficiency and reduced extractive proposal spam around large treasuries.

* **Permissioned coordination models** - We noted that alternatives to token‑centric coordination—such as Holochain’s agent‑centric architecture—enable collaboration without gas, staking, or token dependencies. This opens new patterns for community membership, including permissioned “membranes” that can be tuned to the needs of each group. These models lower onboarding friction and make participation feel closer to normal software usage.

* **Onboarding through belonging** - We emphasized that DAOs grow when they feel open, welcoming, and culturally rich. For Beefy, that meant maintaining non‑judgmental spaces where people can engage across interests rather than policing a narrow definition of what a DAO “should” be. Community belonging, not just financial incentives, was presented as the real catalyst for long‑term adoption.

* **Empowerment via contribution** - We underscored the role of shared values, shared meaning, and psychological ownership in sustaining participation. When contributors feel they shaped the system—through art, culture, or governance—they are more likely to stay invested. Empowerment, not just capital, is what turns a DAO into a durable institution.

* **Delegate programme limits** - We assessed delegate programmes as useful stopgaps for voter apathy but warned they can centralize power, ossify early popularity, and create a “meta‑delegate” class across multiple DAOs. Without mechanisms for ongoing re‑delegation and accountability, delegates risk becoming another bottleneck. The panel suggested treating them as temporary tools while better governance models mature.
