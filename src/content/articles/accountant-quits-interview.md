In July 2025, we appeared on The Accountant Quits podcast for Episode 93 — a deep dive into onchain treasury management, Investment Policy Statements (IPS), non‑custodial asset management, and DAO financial reporting. Below is our executive summary of the conversation and its key takeaways.

---

## Executive Summary

* **Self‑custody with support** - We explored why many crypto projects want the benefits of self‑custody without the burden of actively managing large treasuries, and how asset managers like kpk support that balance with non‑custodial infrastructure which allows for asset management without handing over custody. We see curated and transparent onchain asset management as a response to DAO needs rather than a replacement for decentralized control.

* **IPSs for alignment** - We reflected on the increasing adoption of Investment Policy Statements (**IPSs**), emphasising that IPSs are not novel DeFi inventions but long‑standing tools from traditional finance that define roles, responsibilities, and expectations. DAOs often skip this important alignment step, which later makes disputes over accountability harder to resolve. An IPS starts with a candid audit of where assets are, how cash flows move, and what goals the organization shares. Policies then narrow from high‑level principles into more specific parameters such as acceptable burn rates, asset exposure limits, or whitelist and blacklist rules, depending on the organization’s maturity and needs.

* **Values‑first IPS** - We highlighted the Ethereum Foundation’s 2025 work on a values‑first IPS, which sets clear financial targets while signaling a broader DeFi direction rooted in privacy, verified contracts, and decentralization. This reframes the IPS as a statement of industry values, not just a list of permissible investments. The Ethereum Foundation has blazed a trail for wider ecosystem alignment, and demonstrates the power of a strong IPS to bring stakeholders together.

* **Two‑arm model** - We described the structure of kpk’s asset management business as two complementary verticals: a core asset management arm and a technology product arm that automates best practices. The methodology spans values and scoping, operational execution (treasury, security/infrastructure, and ecosystem growth), and governance, transparency, and reporting, creating a framework that can be tailored to each DAO.

* **Permissioned delegation** - We discussed the principal‑agent tension in DeFi and how non‑custodial management works to bypass these difficulties and avoid the need for trust. Non-custodial management uses permissioned smart contracts (e.g., Zodiac Roles Modifier) and a permissions layer to enable external managers to handle client assets without taking custody. The permissions layer sets granular, auditable limits, allowing DAOs to retain execution authority while delegating operational expertise.

* **Reporting tradeoffs** - We contrasted the range of different approaches to financial reporting across the Web 3.0 industry, from standardised monthly reporting to bespoke, narrative‑heavy reporting. kpk’s reports prioritize consistency and cross‑DAO comparability, while Beefy’s quarterly prioritize depth, custom categories, and investor‑level storytelling, illustrating tradeoffs between accessibility, detail, and standardisation.

* **Automation with Octav** - We explained how portfolio management software like Octav enables sustainable reporting by shifting from manual block‑explorer work to automated labeling, rules‑based categorization, and API‑driven aggregation. This reduced uncategorized transactions dramatically and created a direct pipeline from on‑chain data to financial statements without traditional accounting software.
